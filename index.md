  working on data-driven dynamical systems, Koopman eigenfunctions, and C. elegans neural dynamics.

I am an applied mathematician who studies how collective behavior arises in networked dynamical
systems. My work combines dynamical systems theory with data-driven modeling.
In neuroscience, I build models of the nematode *C.&nbsp;elegans*, constrained by its connectome and
fit to whole-brain imaging, that show how its neural circuits switch the animal between behaviors.
I also develop methods for identifying governing equations from data, particularly when data are
scarce. In other work, I use Koopman eigenfunctions to find exact solutions of nonlinear
differential equations.
[More about my research &rarr;](/research/)

Before joining Illinois Tech in 2024, I was an NSF Mathematical Sciences Postdoctoral Research
Fellow at the Courant Institute, New York University, working with
[Lai-Sang Young](https://math.nyu.edu/~lsy/). I received my Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics from the
University of Washington in 2021, advised by [J. Nathan Kutz](https://faculty.washington.edu/kutz/),
and my B.S. in Applied Mathematics and Behavioral Neuroscience from Western Washington University.
I am an affiliate member of the [National Institute for Theory and Mathematics in Biology](https://www.nitmb.org/) (NITMB).
